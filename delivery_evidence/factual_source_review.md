# Independent factual source review

Review access date: **2026-11-10**. These are source checks, not a certification of every editorial interpretation.

1. **UNESCO World Heritage Centre — Venice and its Lagoon**  
   https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/394/  
   Page text states that Venice is “spread over 118 small islands.” Supports the 118-island graphic and the conservative “more than a hundred islands” narration. UNESCO/ICOMOS documentation also names the three lagoon inlets/gates: Lido, Malamocco and Chioggia: https://whc.unesco.org/document/153362

2. **Italian Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport / Venice Water Authority — The Mose: What is it and how does it work**  
   https://www.fincosit.it/fincosit/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/glf_mose.pdf  
   Project material states protection from tides up to 3 m and describes water-filled gates resting on the seabed, then rising after compressed air empties them. This supports **300 cm as a design protection level**, not a measured flood. The design configuration pages enumerate 21 + 20 Lido gates, 19 Malamocco gates and 18 Chioggia gates (78 total). This is dated project material, not current operating certification.

3. **Lionello, Nicholls, Umgiesser & Zanchettin (2021), Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences**  
   “Venice flooding and sea level: past evolution, present issues, and future projections,” published 2021-09-01.  
   https://nhess.copernicus.org/articles/21/2633/2021/  
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.5194/nhess-21-2633-2021  
   States that the 4 November 1966 event reached the highest recorded water height of 194 cm; that relative sea level combines mean sea-level rise and natural/anthropogenic subsidence; and that the subsidence contribution accelerated during 1950–1970. The article says regulation and alternative water supplies have avoided the anthropogenic subsidence since the 1970s, so “Venice still sinks” needs the narrower interpretation of continuing natural/local subsidence rather than an unchanged pumping-driven trend.

4. **CEDIM / Karlsruhe Institute of Technology — Flooding in Venice, Italy (Report No. 1, information as of 2019-11-19)**  
   Bernhard Mühr, Susanna Mohr & Michael Kunz.  
   https://www.cedim.kit.edu/download/FDA_VENICE_2019_draft_e.pdf  
   Confirms the 1966 level of 194 cm. **Important discrepancy:** this cited source defines the acqua-alta/flood threshold as **80 cm**, not 90 cm. Its table lists 90 cm as a level at which 2% of the city is flooded. Therefore the repository’s ledger assertion that this source verifies a “90 cm reference threshold” is not supported by the cited text. The 90 cm value is not a flood peak and should be relabelled with a precise, current definition/source or changed.

5. **BBC Future — Mud, water and wood: The system that kept a 1604-year-old city afloat**  
   Anna Bressanin, published 2025-03-26.  
   https://www.bbc.com/future/article/20250324-the-ancient-forest-that-supports-venice  
   Supports the broad “millions of wooden piles” description while explicitly saying the exact number is unknown. It also reports expert findings that anaerobic bacteria do attack the wood; the system persists because bacterial action is slow and water-filled cells plus wood/soil/water maintain the structure. Thus the narration’s absolute “the timber never rotted” is too strong. “It hardened instead” is not substantiated by this source and risks repeating the oversimplified petrification claim.

6. **MOSE operational success — Designboom report, updated 2020-12-07**  
   https://www.designboom.com/architecture/mose-flood-barrier-venice-storm-alex-10-05-2020/  
   Reports successful operation on 3 October 2020 and subsequent protection events. This supports the limited statement that the shield has worked in real events, not that it guarantees permanent or universal protection.

7. **“Most beautiful city ever built on water” and “only the first of many”**  
   These are subjective/rhetorical editorial statements, not objectively verifiable quantities. The closing map identifies other exposed cities, but no sourced comparative ranking or unique chronological claim is established. They should be framed as rhetoric rather than fact.

## 241 cm and 300 cm conclusion

- **241 cm:** no source value and not present in the latest chart. The repository history/report identifies it as a transient intermediate in the old 0→300 spring counter. The latest `Sample60.tsx` holds numeric labels fixed while animating bar geometry. No 241 cm claim should be treated as a measured statistic.
- **300 cm:** verified as the MOSE **design protection limit of 3 m** by official project material. It is not a historical flood measurement. The latest chart correctly labels the category “MOSE LIMIT,” but the large “300” numeral crowds/clips against the upper chart region in sampled frames around 37 and 41 seconds.
